Root Touch Up: How Often Do You Really Need One?

Fresh colour looks great, but within a few weeks a line of new growth starts to appear at the parting. For anyone covering greys or wearing a shade that's quite different from their natural colour, a root touch up is the easiest way to keep colour looking fresh without recolouring all of your hair.

This guide explains how often most people need one, what affects the timing, the signs that it's time to book, and simple ways to stretch the time between appointments.

What Is a Root Touch Up?

A root touch up colours only the new growth near your scalp, to blend with your existing shade and cover greys. The rest of your hair, which is already coloured, is left alone.

That makes it:

  • Quicker than colouring your whole head
  • Gentler on your lengths, since they aren't being coloured again
  • The natural way to maintain global colour, which always shows a visible root line as it grows

At Xing Premium Unisex Salon, root touch-ups are available in a regular formula and an ammonia-free version.

How Often Do You Need a Root Touch Up?

Most people book a root touch up every 4–6 weeks. Where you fall in that range depends on a few things.

How fast your hair grows

Everyone's hair grows at a slightly different rate. If yours grows quickly, regrowth shows sooner and you'll lean towards the shorter end of the range.

How many greys you have

Greys are the main reason people book regular touch-ups. The more grey you have, and the more it contrasts with your colour, the sooner a root line shows.

The contrast between your colour and your natural shade

A shade close to your natural base grows out softly. A shade that's much darker or lighter than your natural colour shows a sharper line, sooner.

Your haircut and parting

Short cuts, a centre parting and sleek, pulled-back styles show the roots more. Longer, layered or side-parted styles can hide regrowth for a little longer.

When Is It Time for a Root Touch Up?

You don't need to count weeks. Watch for these signs instead:

  • A visible line of different colour at your parting
  • Greys showing at the hairline or temples
  • Colour near the roots looking flat or different from your lengths
  • You find yourself changing your parting to hide regrowth

If you notice them regularly at a certain point after colouring, that's your natural touch-up rhythm.

Root Touch Up vs Global Colour

Ammonia-Free Root Touch Ups

If you prefer a gentler formula, ask about an ammonia-free root touch up. It colours your regrowth to blend with your shade, with an ammonia-free formula. It's a popular choice for people who colour frequently and want a gentler option. Read more in our guide to ammonia-free hair colour.

If it's your first time colouring or you have sensitive skin, ask us about a patch test before your appointment.

Root Touch Ups for Men

Men's grey coverage follows the same rules. Short haircuts often show regrowth faster, simply because there's less length to hide it. A regular root touch up or global colour keeps grey coverage natural rather than patchy, and many men also colour their beard to match. Our men's grooming services include global colour, ammonia-free options, highlights and beard colour.

How Can You Stretch the Time Between Visits?

You can't stop hair growing, but you can make your colour look fresher for longer.

Look after your colour

Colour that fades quickly makes regrowth look more obvious. A colour-safe routine helps:

  • Use a colour-safe, sulphate-free shampoo
  • Wash with cool or lukewarm water
  • Use a heat protectant before styling
  • Protect your hair from strong sun and chlorine

Our guide on how to make hair colour last longer goes into detail.

Change your parting

Switching to a side parting or a zig-zag parting can soften the look of regrowth for a few days.

Choose a shade close to your natural base

If you're constantly fighting regrowth, ask your stylist about a shade closer to your natural colour. The grow-out will be softer and less obvious.

Consider softer techniques

For some people, adding highlights or a soft, blended technique around a global colour helps disguise regrowth. Your stylist can suggest whether this suits your hair and how many greys you have.

What Happens at a Root Touch Up Appointment

At Xing, every hair colour service starts with a consultation:

  1. Consultation: your stylist checks your regrowth, your current shade and how your lengths look.
  2. Shade matching: the colour is matched to your existing shade, so the roots blend in.
  3. Application to the roots only.
  4. Processing and rinse.
  5. Condition, style and aftercare tips.

If your lengths look faded, your stylist may suggest refreshing them as well, or adding a hair spa or Olaplex to keep coloured hair healthy.

Salon Touch-Up vs Home Colour

Box colour at home can seem quicker, but it has drawbacks:

  • It's hard to apply only to the roots, so colour often overlaps onto the lengths and builds up.
  • Shade matching is tricky, and roots can end up a different tone from the rest of your hair.
  • Greys at the hairline and crown are easy to miss when you can't see them clearly.

A salon root touch up is matched to your existing shade and applied only where it's needed. If you have used home colour, tell your stylist so they can plan around it.

Common Root Touch Up Mistakes

  • Waiting too long. The longer you wait, the more obvious the line and the harder it is to blend.
  • Colouring the lengths every time. Recolouring already-coloured lengths again and again can dry them out. A root touch up keeps the lengths healthier.
  • Using harsh shampoos. Fading lengths make the roots look even more different.
  • Choosing a shade far from your natural colour if low maintenance is your goal.

A Quick Root Touch Up Checklist

  • Notice your regrowth pattern and book before the line becomes obvious
  • Ask about ammonia-free if you prefer a gentler formula
  • Use colour-safe, sulphate-free products
  • Tell your stylist about any home colour or henna
  • Refresh your lengths only when they need it

Key Takeaways

  • Most people book a root touch up every 4–6 weeks, depending on growth and how visible their regrowth is.
  • Greys, a big contrast between your natural and coloured shade, and short or parted styles make regrowth show sooner.
  • A root touch up colours only the new growth, so it's quicker and gentler on your lengths than a full global colour.
  • Ammonia-free root touch-ups are available if you prefer a gentler formula.
  • Colour-safe care and smart styling can stretch the time between visits a little.
